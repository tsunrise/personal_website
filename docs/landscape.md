# Landscape editing guide

The background is an original, procedural blue-dusk Suzhou landscape: moon initially at upper right, misty mountains, river, stone arch bridge with softly washed banks, willow at left, and reeds in the shallows. The moon can be dragged through the sky, including behind mountains, and its light follows its position. Keep the center open for the small serif title, links, and questions. Movement should remain quiet and secondary to reading.

## Rendering model

`paintLandscape(w, h)` returns ordered `Painting` objects: a canvas, a parallax `depth`, a `kind`, and optional `windMap`, `shadowMap`, `normalMap`, and `fallback` canvases. The river also carries a `waveBoundary` float field. Lighting metadata includes `lightStrength` and `twoSided`; mountain layers also expose the exact painted `ridge` polyline. The moon is a compact sprite containing its disc and halo. The river's fallback canvas contains static ripples; direct moon highlights are computed from the current light instead of baked into either river canvas. The main drawing uses `seeded(41)`; changing the seed or random-call order changes subsequent details. Bridge materials use `seeded(127)`; banks use `seeded(289)`. The main sequence preserves the established random consumption for the bridge/banks and old moon highlights so plants remain unchanged. Base artwork is generated on initialization and resize, not on each frame or chat update; moon transforms and lighting update independently.

Three.js uses an orthographic camera, a movable moon sprite, and transparent full-screen landscape planes. Their 1.055 overscan hides edges during movement and is included in moon projection. Depth testing is disabled: `renderOrder`, not physical Z, controls overlap. Painting order is moon, mountains, river, bank contact marks, bridge, banks, reed shallows, reeds, willow wood, then foliage. The shader sky is order 0, transparent clouds 1.25 (after the moon, before mountains), geese 1.5, and mist 5.5 (after the river, before the bank contact marks). Clouds are not baked into the opaque sky or multiplied into the moon sprite; their alpha covers the disc and halo exactly once. Recheck these explicit orders if adding or moving layers.

`kind` selects shader behavior:

- `moon`: movable sprite, with mountain occlusion aligned to the painted ridges.
- `paint` → 0: painterly texture plus grain and optional normal-based moonlight.
- `water` → 1: moving mountain reflections, ripples, and glints, with a soft bridge light-occlusion mask.
- `willow` → 2 and `reeds` → 3: world-space bending followed by perspective projection. Wind maps store flexibility in R (0 pins a point, 1 allows full movement), camera depth / 16 m in G, and vertical rest distance from the attachment / 3 m in B. Keep these textures in `NoColorSpace`.
- `shallows` → 4: bank contact marks and reed-bed reflections use the river's wave distortion without adding a second layer of sky/moon lighting.

Canvas coordinates run downward from the top; shader UV Y runs upward. `WATER_HORIZON = 0.295` in `composition.ts` supplies both the Canvas horizon (`h * 0.705`) and water shader. Plants and water share a virtual camera with `EYE_HEIGHT = 1.4` m and principal UV `(0.5, WATER_HORIZON)`. `moonPosition()` supplies the responsive default moon position and radius. The current moon state supplies its displayed position and shared light direction/radius. Portrait composition switches at `w / h < 0.85`; positions are recomputed, not merely cropped.

## Moon interaction and lighting

The whole moon disc stays inside the visible viewport sky, above the water horizon; mountain ridgelines do not constrain dragging. Mouse and single-finger touch use pointer capture and retain the initial grab offset. Movement beyond 6 CSS pixels is a drag. The circular target remains over the entire disc even when mountains completely hide it. A click or tap returns the moon to its responsive default only when mountain coverage is strictly greater than 90%; the return takes 650 ms and can be interrupted by dragging. Arrow keys move the focused moon and Home returns it to default. Its transparent hit target has a minimum 44-pixel size and a visible keyboard focus ring. Foreground text, links, composer, footer, and dialogs keep input priority.

Moon position lives only in memory. Each page load starts at the existing responsive default; resize retains a moved position and clamps it to the new sky bounds. Align the artwork, hit target, mountain coverage, and lighting through the same coordinate transforms, including the 1.055 plane overscan and each layer's parallax. Do not infer coverage from the moon's center alone or include its halo in the disc-area calculation.

The bottom-left 月読 toggle enables Tsukuyomi mode in `App.tsx`. It starts off on every page load. While active, page content and the chat dimming overlay are hidden, leaving the landscape, moon interaction, and toggle available. Switching modes keeps the landscape mounted, preserving moon position and animation state; drafts and answers return when leaving the mode. The toggle is translucent when inactive and fully opaque when active.

The moon is a distant directional source: dragging changes direction and atmospheric elevation, never local inverse-square falloff. Every receiver uses the same water-camera ray, including overscan and layer parallax. The large painted disc is intentional; its camera-derived angular size broadens reflections without multiplying total illumination. `sampleLighting()` models exponential extinction through a spherical atmosphere (Earth radius 6,371 km, effective shell thickness 8 km, vertical optical depth 0.10), normalized against the clear responsive default moon. The finite shell avoids singular air mass at the horizon.

Incident intensity is the atmosphere-filtered light before ground obstruction; clouds receive this for backlighting. Direct intensity multiplies incident intensity by the jointly transmitted disc fraction: 32 vertical chords, with four cloud samples inside each mountain-exposed segment, weighted by exposed area and normalized by the **whole** disc. Do not multiply separate mountain/cloud averages; clouds over an already-hidden portion must not dim exposed light again. Sky sampling uses depth 0, terrain coverage each ridge's own depth, and source rays the depth-3 water camera. The weighted visible rays provide a source centroid, tangent basis, and angular covariance for water. The independent 256-chord mountain-only coverage still controls the strictly-greater-than-90% reset threshold.

Weather frames update transmitted intensity and source moments even with a stationary moon. Cache mountain-mask generation, hit-target positioning, and bridge-shadow geometry independently of moving cloud opacity. Diffuse surfaces use the geometric moon direction; water uses the exposed source centroid. Normal maps remain linear RGB metadata, with camera axes +X right, +Y up, +Z away. Solid surfaces use `max(N·L, 0)`; foliage adds only `0.3 × max(−N·L, 0)` for subdued transmission. Decode painted sRGB into linear light before applying the shared calibrated gain, then encode for display. The gain divides ambient-plus-direct response by the clear default response, preserving the original painting under the reference moon. Ambient gain ranges from 0.88 to 1.0 and changes smoothly with direct intensity and elevation, including sky, mist, and artwork without normal metadata. Keep low-light terrain readable and highlights quiet.

The moon sprite also needs atmospheric **airlight**, independently of how much direct light reaches the ground. Both renderers transport its authored linear contrast as `currentSky + incidentIntensity × (authoredMoon − referenceSky)`, with a nonnegative radiance bound. Sample the cloud-free current sky along each sprite pixel and the clear reference sky at its matching pixel around the default moon. This preserves the reference painting and lets the disc and halo lose contrast into the local sky near the horizon instead of becoming an opaque dark cutout. Keep sprite alpha unchanged; apply mountains and clouds in their existing passes exactly once. This is a calibrated approximation to [transmitted surface radiance plus in-scattered atmospheric light](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_II_Volume_Rendering/The_Equation_of_Transfer), using the painted sky rather than a spectral atmosphere solver. It does not simulate wavelength-dependent lunar reddening.

`getCloudAtlas()` creates one deterministic 512×512 atlas per module lifetime; R/G encode two shallow optical-depth layers, not color or alpha. CPU bilinear sampling matches repeat-wrapped, linear-filtered GPU sampling with no color conversion or mipmaps. Both layers follow integrated wind displacement. Cloud alpha is `1 − exp(−(nearDepth + farDepth))`. Relative sheets around depths 1 and 1.06, with four directional samples through the farther layer, approximate soft inter-layer shadows. Thin bodies scatter the dusk ambient light into pale pearl washes, shading exponentially toward blue interiors as optical depth and inter-layer shadow increase. This ambient contribution keeps some clouds visible at startup even away from the moon. Thin illuminated regions additionally receive restrained forward-scattered silver light. This is a shallow two-layer transport approximation, not a volumetric cloud solver. The same sky/cloud field supplies reflected water radiance and Canvas fallback.

The Canvas 2D fallback retains moon interaction, joint occlusion, linear diffuse lighting, ambient adjustment, cloud coverage/backlighting, and static water reflection. Its weather field freezes when fallback starts; moving the moon relights that same frozen field. Do not run full-scene CPU repainting during WebGL animation or read back GPU pixels to determine moon transmission.

## Bridge and banks

The design takes its intimate garden scale from the tiny arch in Suzhou's [Master-of-Nets Garden](https://en.chinaculture.org/focus/focus/cities/2010-04/22/content_377545.htm), with the quiet stone silhouette and reflection of the Chinese-influenced Engetsu-kyo in [Koishikawa Korakuen](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/24/index.html) as a second reference. It is an original interpretation, not a reconstruction. An image-generation concept was used for visual exploration only; all final scenery is drawn in code with no raster assets.

`createBridgeGeometry()` projects a uniformly scaled, slightly oblique bridge through the river's camera. Preserve its world proportions across aspect ratios: portrait views crop the far approach rather than stretching the arch. Desktop placement exposes both landings; portrait placement shifts right to leave the existing left reed beds in water. A single circular segment defines the opening, barrel, and radial voussoirs, with constant ring thickness and masonry above the crown. Low continuous parapets, attached terminal stones, level treads, vertical risers, and a level crown landing make the structure legible. The rendering is deliberately painterly: three main tonal planes, broad faint mineral blooms, dry-brush striations, seven partial radial joint marks, and an interrupted coping highlight. Keep the barrel a single quiet blue shadow; do not restore the masonry grid, outlined barrel facets, bright bevels, or intermediate parapet posts. Precise projected construction supplies depth while sparse marks match the mountains and plants.

There is no mirrored bridge image on the water. Stone-contact marks use a separate depth-4 `shallows` layer beneath the solid bridge and banks. Keep both abutments embedded in land. The near shore sweeps in from the lower right as a broad crescent, opening an inlet toward the central water; the far headland recedes to the right edge. Use layered blue-green earth washes and a few grouped, rounded footing stones. Avoid narrow parallel-sided approaches, repeated rock edging, and lawn-like texture. Keep the arch opening free of bank fill and move reed beds with the shoreline so their roots stay in open shallows.

`paintBridgeWaterShadow()` accepts the current light direction and projects the walls, deck, and barrel along that ray, intersecting `y = 0` before applying the shared perspective. Clip projected polygons against the camera near plane and viewport before perspective division so a low moon cannot produce enormous inverted shadows. Union the face silhouettes into a light-occlusion mask, then soften it with a blur of 0.4 times the projected model scale and fade toward the foreground. Reuse cached construction silhouettes and scratch canvases as the river's `shadowMap` changes. Its shader attenuates direct water lighting and glints while retaining ambient water color and ripples. A shared `uShadowOffset` compensates for depth-3 water versus depth-4 bridge parallax. The Canvas 2D fallback uses the same current mask and light visibility. The later bridge and bank layers occlude the shaded water naturally. Do not substitute a mirrored or displaced copy of the bridge image, introduce a colored silhouette overlay, or change the plant random sequence.

Portrait placement starts from world scale 0.52 (desktop uses 0.65), then retains 62.5% of the near approach's projected height above the viewport bottom. Scaling the world model and camera distance together preserves the apparent bridge span and proportions. The light-occlusion mask and bank contacts follow the shared camera projection. Portrait banks widen at the foot and shoulder while retaining those contacts. A lower, broader inner wash and shorter diagonal mineral strokes keep the bank from reading as a vertically stretched mound. Six mobile reed beds follow the inlet at staggered depths, including a close group in the open shallows beneath the arch rather than an isolated far-water group. Keep their roots clear of both shores and masonry, including at 320×800, and subtract the bank silhouette from mobile reed reflections so they do not spill onto dry land. Desktop coordinates, reed counts, and the plant random sequence remain unchanged.

## Wind and water

The wind is a lightweight procedural approximation of sheltered summer weather, not a fluid solver. World +X is screen-right and +Z is across the river away from the viewer. `wind.ts` draws a fresh 32-bit weather seed and a prevailing direction over the full circle once per page load. The profile lives in module memory, independently of the artwork seed, so React remounts/Strict Mode, hidden tabs, and resize do not choose another night; nothing is saved to browser storage. Pass an explicit `BreezeProfile` to `sampleBreeze()` for reproducible checks. Quintic seeded noise combines slow weather (19 s), gusts (5.7 s), and small fluctuations (1.9 s); direction veers more slowly (37/11 s), within ±0.62 radians of that night's prevailing direction. The bounded speed is 0.42–2.08 m/s, around light air/light breeze. See the [NWS Beaufort scale](https://www.weather.gov/mfl/beaufort) for the calm ripple/wavelet reference.

One persistent `createWind()` instance advances at fixed 1/120 s steps within the existing render clock. It survives resize, freezes while the page is hidden, and never catches up on wall-clock time. Moon return animation also excludes hidden time. Clouds and mist use the integral of the same velocity (at different projection scales); subtract this displacement in texture lookups so they travel downwind. Do not substitute `currentSpeed * time`, which jumps when the speed changes.

Willow and reed bending is driven by quadratic drag (`velocity * speed`), with separate damped springs; reeds respond faster. Willow/reed damping ratios of 0.50/0.58 allow gentle recoil after gusts, with world-space flexibility gains of 0.0365/0.0325 m per unit drag. Natural angular frequencies of 2.15/3.27 rad/s give these softer stems a slightly longer sway period. The willow's spatial loading is centered on 0.60 (reeds: 1.00), keeping its hanging foliage closer to vertical even in the initial breeze. Both retain ±0.26 spatial load variation and smaller wind-aligned leaf flutter to break up synchronized movement. Increase responsiveness through these plant parameters rather than increasing the shared weather's speed. Flexibility masks pin roots/attachments; the willow's 88-pixel mask strokes cover the swept leaves so larger bends do not clip at the resting silhouette. Wood stays still.

The wind vector has two horizontal world components (X/Z), with no vertical gust. Plant points reconstruct their resting camera-space position from UV and mask depth, bend in world X/Z, then project using the new depth. Wind away from the viewer shrinks the foliage toward the horizon's vanishing point; wind toward the viewer enlarges it. This also makes the same sideways displacement subtler on farther reeds. Reed depth is inferred from each clump's waterline root; the foreground willow uses a shallow 4.6–5.56 m canopy depth gradient in its uncropped local coordinates. A root-to-point reach constraint gives upright reeds a small downward bend and hanging willow tips a small upward arc, capped at 28% lateral deflection. Two inverse-lookup iterations sample the deformed artwork without moving its pinned roots. This is a lightweight perspective deformation of painted layers, not a full plant mesh: overlap order stays fixed and individual leaves do not turn to expose a back face. It adds no textures or per-frame CPU work. See [Khronos on perspective division](https://wikis.khronos.org/opengl/Perspective_Divide).

Water velocity and energy relax toward the wind over 7 s, retaining motion through a lull; surface drift is 1.5% of this filtered wind.

The river uses six fixed directional gravity/capillary waves, with `omega² = (9.81*k + 0.000074*k³) * tanh(k*1.8)` and millimetre-scale amplitudes. `uWaveBasis` orients the spectrum to the initial breeze, allowing nights with wind from any direction. This basis stays fixed for the scene's lifetime, including resize: subsequent wind alignment controls energy, not wavevector rotation, so veering does not swivel existing ripples. A perspective ray/plane intersection compresses distant waves, and pixel-footprint filtering suppresses unresolved ripples. Analytic slopes distort the painted mountain reflections and provide normals for Fresnel sky reflection and moon specular reflection. This follows the small spectral-surface approach in [GPU Gems, Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models).

Waves interact with the river's edges in `waveBoundary.ts`. After the bridge and banks are painted, `createWaveBoundary()` converts the bank waterlines, stone contact footprints, and the bridge abutments' world rectangles into the water camera's metres and samples a 160-column field (rows from the viewport bottom up; skipped above the horizon). Each texel stores the soft wall normal pointing into the water, the distance to the nearest edge, and reflectance: masonry 0.9, stones 0.5, sloping earth banks 0.28. Normals are a length-weighted soft minimum over edges within 7 × 0.22 m of the nearest one; equidistant walls cancel to zero confidence along a channel's centre line instead of exposing a seam, and convex corners radiate normals. Confidence fades from 3 m to the 5 m reach. Landscape uploads it once per rebuild as a linearly filtered `HalfFloatType` `DataTexture` (8-bit distance quantizes the image phase) shared by the river and both shallows layers; `uBoundaryParallax = 4 − depth` aligns it with the depth-4 bridge through `uShadowOffset`.

Per spectral component, `boundaryWave()` and its GLSL twin apply the method of images. A wave approaching an edge (`direction·n < 0`) adds a mirrored copy (`k − 2(k·n)n`) whose phase is the incident phase at the image point `x − 2dn`. At a fully reflecting wall this doubles elevation and cancels the normal slope, the no-flux condition `∂η/∂n = 0`, so crests bend into a standing clapotis beside the abutments. The reflected beam is scaled by reflectance and confidence, enters smoothly from grazing incidence, and decays over `0.6 + 1.2λ` metres as a finite wall's beam diffracts. A component leaving an edge cannot have crossed the land: its amplitude regrows from zero as `1 − exp(−fetch / (2.5λ))`, with `fetch = d / (direction·n)`, giving calmer water in the lee of the bridge and the upwind shore while short ripples recover first. Gains are continuous through grazing incidence, and open water beyond the reach is unchanged. This is a local image/fetch approximation, not a wave-equation solver; it omits shoaling, refraction over the bank slope, and breaking. Visibility depends on that night's wind: from this low camera, crest bending shows best where wind drives waves onto the abutments or the inlet shore. The Canvas fallback keeps its static ripples.

`waterLighting.ts` evaluates a normalized anisotropic Beckmann slope distribution, water Fresnel reflectance, and Smith masking using intrinsic water roughness. Visible-disc angular covariance is projected through the half-vector slope Jacobian and convolved with unresolved surface slopes; masking uses surface roughness alone. This moment-matched Gaussian source approximation preserves flux while partial occlusion changes the reflection's centroid and width; it does not reconstruct an exact clipped-disc outline or lunar phase. Multiply by transmitted direct intensity once, without another source-area factor. Reflected sky/cloud color follows the same ambient state, while bridge shadow attenuates direct moonlight without erasing ambient water and wave detail. Reflection placement follows ray geometry: a high moon's strongest flat-water reflection can lie below the viewport. Keep the near-water highlight quiet rather than moving a fixed bright strip across the river.

## Details to preserve

- **Mountains:** fade each painted layer's alpha smoothly from 8.5% of viewport height above the water horizon to 3.5% below it. This dissolves low ridge contours into the mist behind the bridge while retaining the higher silhouettes; a color wash alone leaves a traced edge.
- **Willow:** the tree uses a local width of `max(w, h * 1.15)` and shifts left by 26% of the excess width. Narrow screens crop the canopy instead of squeezing it; wood, foliage, and wind masks share this translation. Do not reintroduce X-only scaling. `grow()` samples parent branches so joints remain connected. The woody skeleton is static; foliage is a separate layer. Leaf positions follow their hanging stem curves, and wind masks pin attachments even where masks overlap.
- **Reeds:** keep roots in shallow water, not on dry banks. Submerged stems fade into soft bed shadows and broken reflections. Root masks stay dark so tips sway without the whole clump sliding across the water.
- **Bridge:** bridge, banks, and contact marks all use depth 4 so parallax cannot separate them. The shadow mask is sampled by the water shader with the corresponding parallax offset. Keep the earth approaches connected to both landings and water contact marks below shoreline rocks.
- **River:** preserve the alpha fade into distant mist; a hard texture edge previously produced a horizontal seam. Mountain silhouettes mirror about the shared horizon, clip to the water below it, and receive a faint dark blue wash. Its opacity starts at zero with a smooth onset at the horizon, then fades toward the foreground; a nonzero first stop creates a straight seam. Do not copy the pale mountain colors directly: their bright inverted fills made the darker gaps look like upright submerged mountains. Both WebGL and the static fallback use the same reflected wash.
- **Parallax:** depth is approximately maximum displacement in CSS pixels. Current values run from 2 for the moon to 5 for the willow. Keep tree and bridge motion subtle; text stays stationary.
- **Geese:** 2–6 birds per flock, with individual wingbeats and brief glides. First arrival is after 12–24 animation seconds; flights last 18–28 seconds, followed by 55–120 quiet seconds. The schedule survives resize and uses the shared animation clock rather than wall-clock timers.

## Performance and fallback

Rendering targets 60 fps through requestAnimationFrame, with a cadence-preserving cap on higher-refresh displays and a 1 ms timestamp tolerance. Actual frame rate depends on device performance. Device pixel ratio is capped at 1.5. Painting resolution scales by `min(1.5, 1600 / width, 1400 / height)`; resize is debounced by 160 ms. Mouse parallax requires a fine pointer. Single-finger touchstart/touchmove use the same clamped viewport coordinates through passive listeners, so Safari native scrolling can continue after pointer cancellation. Touch release, cancellation, or multiple fingers return the parallax target to center. The moon target alone uses `touch-action: pinch-zoom` for deliberate dragging; scrolling elsewhere and native pinch zoom remain available. Animation runs while the page is visible; there is no manual pause control or saved pause preference. System reduced-motion preferences are not consulted. Hidden tabs do not advance the clock or render.

WebGL initialization failure or context loss switches to `drawFallback()`, which composites the same artwork over a static sky. It has no shader movement or geese and remains active until remount/reload. Both initialization paths clamp the painting dimensions to at least one pixel, including a hidden or not-yet-measured host. Inspect `.landscape[data-renderer]` (`webgl` or `canvas2d`) when debugging.

Register new textures/materials for disposal, including wind, shadow, normal, moon occlusion, wave-boundary, and cloud optical-depth maps. The immutable CPU cloud atlas survives rebuilds; each renderer's GPU texture is disposed normally. Dispose bird geometry, cancel animation frames and resize timers, disconnect observers, release pointer capture, and remove listeners on teardown. Resizing rebuilds textures but must not restart the flock schedule, reset a moved moon, change the cloud field, or leak GPU resources.
